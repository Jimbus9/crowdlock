The documents

Crowdlock is these documents, not any piece of software. Anyone holding them can build their own app, their own register, or take part with neither.

Six of them are general and can be used as they stand. The seventh is the declaration, which each Crowdlock writes for itself.

  1. 1

    The declaration

    What a Crowdlock is for and what it will never do. Each Crowdlock writes its own, and its members sign it, so once anybody has signed, the words can never change.

    An example, The Kop Fund's: declaration-v1.txt · v1 · 3,408 bytes · sha256:3a0a0bca…6209b96

  2. 2

    Registration format

    What a membership record is, how to make one, how to check one, and how to submit it without anyone's permission. Two fields: an address and a signature.

    Plain file: registration-format.md · v1

  3. 3

    Counting rule

    How every published figure is worked out, precisely enough that two people reach the same answer from public data. Includes what must never be published, and why.

    Plain file: counting-rule.md · v1

  4. 4

    Lock format

    How locked money is held: the script, the two ways out, the lengths on offer, and the four behaviours that must hold or nothing else can be relied on.

    Plain file: lock-format.md · v1

  5. 5

    Proposal and settlement format

    How locked money is spent. There is no vote: a proposal is funded or it is not. The windows, how settlement is paced, and what happens when a proposal fails.

    Plain file: proposal-and-settlement.md · v1

  6. 6

    Commitment format

    The record by which one person backs one purchase: a single payment, signed by its owner and nobody else, which cannot move a coin until the co-signer adds the second signature.

    Plain file: commitment-format.md · v1

  7. 7

    Published destinations format

    The only addresses locked money may be sent to, and the whole defence against a forged proposal. Entries can only be added, each one is signed, and each waits in public for a period its Crowdlock chooses (seven days is recommended) before a proposal may name it.

    Plain file: destinations-format.md · v1

Running your own covers which of these are general, what your own Crowdlock must publish, what the method is good and bad at, and what every Crowdlock shares. Plain file: running-your-own.md

Take it

Everything here is free to copy, for any club, any sport, or any cause that fits. Copying it is not just allowed, it is the point. Nobody needs permission, nobody needs to tell us, and nothing is owed to anybody if it works.

Write your own declaration and keep the other six documents as they are. They describe a mechanism, not a football club: a crowd committing money towards buying something it means to keep, with nobody ever holding anybody else's money. A pub, a woodland, a local paper, a shuttered factory: same shape.

A Crowdlock is for a crowd that wants the thing itself, and a say in it, not a profit. It pays no financial return. Investments that pay returns have legal rules to follow, to protect the people putting money in, and Crowdlock follows none of them. That is not what it is for. Running your own spells out the rest.

Why documents rather than software

If a Crowdlock were a piece of software, whoever controlled that software would control the Crowdlock. Writing the rules down is how that is prevented.

Everything a program needs is here. The Crowdlock app and any Crowdlock's website are two possible readings of these documents. They are not the thing itself, and a register has to treat a record made by somebody else's software exactly the same as one made by ours.

Checking it all yourself

You need git and Node 22. Run these one line at a time.

git clone https://github.com/Jimbus9/crowdlock
cd crowdlock

# the lock's construction, and the libraries the checks below use
cd method/lock
npm ci
node selftest.mjs

# commitments, and the destinations list that guards them
node commitment.test.mjs

# the taproot form of the same lock, which nothing uses yet
node taproot.test.mjs
cd ../..

# the signature checker, against an independent library
node method/js/verify.test.mjs

# every Crowdlock's own file, and its declaration's fingerprint
node method/js/crowdlock.test.mjs

# a register: every published signature, checked for real
node method/js/register.test.mjs

A declaration's fingerprint can be reproduced from its published text with the rules in the registration format. Nothing here asks to be believed. Reviewing Crowdlock has the full list of tests, and says what is and isn't proven.

What a version means here. A declaration's version is load-bearing: a signature is made over one exact text, so a second version would be published with its reason and members asked whether they wish to sign it, never applied to words they did not read. The other documents are working documents. They were drafted over the week to 24 October 2026 and are all at version 1. Every change since is in the public commit history.
